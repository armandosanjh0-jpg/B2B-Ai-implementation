# Novelle: Sales Outreach Strategy

---

## 1. Outbound Messaging Structure

Every message, regardless of channel, follows one structure:

**Pattern:** Specific observation → relevant pain → concrete outcome → low-friction next step

Do not lead with what Novelle does. Lead with what the prospect is experiencing.

### The three-layer message framework

**Layer 1: The hook (1-2 sentences)**
Reference something specific about their company, role, or situation. Not generic flattery. An actual signal that shows you looked at them.

**Layer 2: The pain point (1-2 sentences)**
Name the gap they are likely sitting in right now. For Novelle, this is almost always one of:
- Subscribed to Claude/ChatGPT, but usage is shallow or siloed to one person
- Leadership wants AI ROI, but nobody owns implementation
- Workflows are inconsistent across the team even though tools exist

**Layer 3: The outcome or proof (1-2 sentences)**
State a concrete result someone like them got, or articulate what changes when implementation is done right. Avoid vague claims. Use specifics: time saved, workflows built, days to live.

**Layer 4: The ask (1 sentence)**
One clear, low-commitment ask. Not "are you interested?" Not "let's hop on a call." Something specific: "Would it be worth a 20-minute conversation to see if there's a fit?"

### Email rules across all angles

- Subject lines: short, specific, slightly unexpected. No hype words.
- Total length: 5-8 sentences max. If you're going longer, you're pitching, not qualifying.
- One ask per email. Not two.
- Follow-up sequence: send at day 0, day 4, day 10. Three touches then stop for 60 days. The day-4 follow-up can be a single sentence asking if the timing is off.
- Never attach anything in a cold email. Link to the services page if needed.

## 3. LinkedIn DM Approach

LinkedIn DMs work differently than email. The bar for reading is lower, but the tolerance for a pitch is also much lower. The goal of a LinkedIn DM is not to close or even to qualify. It is to get a reply.

### The two-message structure

**Message 1: Connect request note (300 char max)**

Do not pitch. Do not explain what you do. Give one reason to connect that is about them.

> "Hi [Name], noticed you lead ops at [Company] and have been posting about AI tooling. Would be good to be connected."

or:

> "Hi [Name], you work in the space I focus on: AI implementation for lean teams. Worth connecting."

**Message 2: First DM after connection (sent within 24 hours)**

One specific observation, one relevant question. No ask for a call yet.

> "Hi [Name], thanks for connecting. Curious: is your team using Claude or ChatGPT consistently across functions, or is it still mostly one or two people? I ask because that's the pattern I usually see at [company size] stage."

Wait for a reply. If they engage, ask one more qualifying question before suggesting a call. If they don't reply after 5 days, send one follow-up:

> "Following up in case this got buried. Happy to share how we've structured this for other [agency/ops/engineering] teams if useful."

### What not to do on LinkedIn

- Do not copy your cold email into a DM. It reads immediately as mass outreach.
- Do not lead with credentials, awards, or client logos.
- Do not use voice messages unless you have an existing relationship.
- Do not tag them in content to manufacture familiarity before connecting.

### Profile hygiene before outreach

Before a prospect accepts your request, they will visit your profile. The profile must pass a 10-second check:
- Headline states what you do and for whom, not a title
- Banner or featured section links to NOVELLE-DOMAIN-TBD
- Recent activity (2+ posts per week) shows you are active in the AI implementation space

## 4. Lead Qualification Criteria

Qualify before you invest time. These criteria prevent wasted calls and mismatched proposals.

### Tier 1: High fit (proceed immediately)

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Team size 5-50 | Sweet spot for the engagement model and pricing |
| Already using Claude, ChatGPT, or similar | Adoption problem exists; budget for tools is proven |
| Has someone who can own AI internally after handoff | Ensures adoption sticks; reduces support burden |
| Has a specific workflow bottleneck they can name | Engagement scope is clear; faster time to value |
| Budget signal above $1,000 (or not price-resistant) | Avoids scope-creep on thin-margin engagements |

### Tier 2: Qualified with conditions

| Signal | Condition |
|---|---|
| Team under 5 people | Right for the $1,000 Sprint tier only; set expectations on scope |
| No AI tools yet | May need to include tool selection in scope; price accordingly |
| Has interest but no named use case | Discovery call is still worth it; use it to surface one |
| Enterprise (50+ people) | Only proceed if they are open to the Custom Operations tier ($10,000+) |

### Tier 3: Disqualify early

| Signal | Why to disqualify |
|---|---|
| "Just exploring" with no timeline | No urgency means no decision; come back in 90 days |
| No one owns AI adoption internally | Implementation will stall post-handoff; not a fit unless they hire first |
| Budget under $1,000 | Below minimum engagement; direct to free resources instead |
| Wants a strategy deck, not implementation | Wrong service; they need a consultant, not an implementer |
| Needs custom software development | Outside scope; this is workflow implementation, not software engineering |

### Qualification questions for the discovery call

Use these four questions to qualify in the first 10 minutes:

1. "What AI tools is your team using right now, and how consistently are they being used?"
2. "Is there one workflow you wish was running faster or more consistently?"
3. "Who on your team would own AI adoption after we hand off?"
4. "What's your timeline for wanting something live?"

If the answers are vague across all four, the prospect is not ready. End the call by offering to reconnect in 60-90 days.

## 5. Targeting: Where to Find High-Fit Leads

### Primary channels for outbound

**LinkedIn search:**
- Job title: "Head of Operations", "Chief of Staff", "Agency Owner", "Founder", "VP of Engineering", "Marketing Director"
- Company size filter: 11-50 employees
- Industry: Marketing/Advertising, Staffing, SaaS, Professional Services, Management Consulting

**Indicators of a warm prospect:**
- Posted about AI tools in the last 30 days
- Company recently hired an ops or project management role (signal of scaling friction)
- Job posting mentions "AI", "automation", or "efficiency" in a non-technical role

**Apollo / Clay prospecting:**
- Filter by company size, industry, and tech stack (companies using Claude Teams or OpenAI API)
- Enrich with LinkedIn activity to score warmth

### Warm inbound priorities

- Anyone who submits the book-a-call form but does not pick a time: follow up within 24 hours
- Anyone who visits pricing more than once (if analytics is configured): trigger a manual outreach sequence

## 6. Weekly Outreach Cadence

| Day | Activity |
|---|---|
| Monday | Research and build prospect list for the week (15-20 contacts) |
| Tuesday | Send cold emails: Batch 1 (5-7 contacts) |
| Wednesday | Send LinkedIn connection requests (10-15 per day max) |
| Thursday | Cold email Batch 2 + follow up on any Day 4 emails from last week |
| Friday | LinkedIn follow-up DMs; review reply quality and iterate subject lines |

Target: 15-20 outbound touches per week to start. Measure reply rate (target above 8%) and call conversion (target above 30% of replies).
